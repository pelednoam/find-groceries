What this is

Every number here comes from people talking on r/boston, r/CambridgeMA and r/Somerville. Nobody visited a store, checked a shelf, or compared a receipt. It is aggregated opinion with the receipts attached — every claim links back to the comment it came from, and you should click through before believing anything surprising.

What it is not

  • Not prices. There is no price data here. "Cheap" means people say it is cheap, which tracks reputation more than this week's shelf.
  • Not a survey. Reddit is not a representative sample of shoppers, and people post more readily about annoyance than satisfaction.
  • Not current. Claims span many years. Older ones are down-weighted, faster for things that change quickly (cleanliness, crowding) than for structural price gaps.
  • Not independent. One thread can contain many voices agreeing with each other. Caps limit how much one document or one author contributes, but a local consensus is still one consensus.

How a number is built

Each claim gets a weight from three things: how directly the text supports it, how old it is, and how the comment was voted. Weights are summed per (store, branch, category), then shrunk toward neutral so a cell with one claim reads closer to 0 than a cell with forty saying the same thing. A score near zero can mean "genuinely mixed" or "barely any evidence" — the claim count next to it tells you which.

Price is scored on a line from cheap to expensive rather than by majority vote, and is left blank entirely when there is too little to tell. One person calling a store cheap is not enough to label it.

One-off events — a closing-down sale, a pandemic stock-out — are tagged at extraction time and excluded, so a liquidation does not make a store look cheap.

Two sources, not averaged

A second source sits beside the Reddit verdict: 135,345 Google Maps ratings for these stores, from a research dataset that ends in September 2021. It is shown on the tab and never merged into the numbers above.

The reason is that the two disagree in a patterned way. Google reads about +0.41 more positive overall, but the gap is concentrated: the big chains people complain about here are rated perfectly fine on Google (Stop & Shop −0.52 against +0.51), while the specialty shops agree to within a few hundredths. Averaging would selectively rehabilitate the stores this corpus is hardest on, and the output would read as new evidence rather than as a change in which source was doing the talking.

There is a second layer. Restrict Google to reviews whose author wrote a paragraph — the population closest to a Reddit comment — and the mean drops 0.36 stars, halving the gap. People tap five stars on the way out and write prose when annoyed. Which Google number you quote is itself a choice, so the tab lets you pick and shows both.

Only counts and averages are published from that dataset. No Google review text, author name or user id appears anywhere in this site or its data file.

Combining the two

The tab above shows the sources apart. The store pages also show them combined, and the way that works is worth stating because the obvious method is wrong.

Google's ratings are not shifted relative to the corpus so much as compressed: they rank the stores nearly the same way (r = +0.86) but occupy 31% of the range, bunched near the top the way star ratings always are. So the correction is not an offset, it is a rescale — — fitted across every store that has both. Two parameters over twenty stores; leave-one-out error against 0.434 for simply guessing the average, so the map predicts stores it has not seen.

A literal per-store offset would have been twenty parameters for twenty stores. It would fit perfectly, erase every disagreement by construction, and hand back a "combined" figure identical to whichever source it was anchored on. The rescale still corrects each store by a different amount — — because the correction depends on where the store sits.

The two are then combined by inverse variance, which decides the mix on its own. Whatever remains unexplained after calibration () is a floor on how precise a Google estimate can be, no matter how many ratings back it — so Reddit keeps almost all the weight wherever it has thousands of claims, and cedes it where it has one. Across the branches that have both, Reddit's share runs from 13% to 93%. Every combined number on this site shows that split beneath it.

Google contributes one figure per shop, so the combination applies to the overall verdict only. There is no Google opinion about a store's produce, and none is invented.
